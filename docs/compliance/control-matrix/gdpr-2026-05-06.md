**Status legend:** **MAPPED** = full coverage with evidence | **PARTIAL** = some coverage, gap noted | **GAP** = no kingdom work yet | **N/A** = genuinely not applicable to a software platform | **DEFERRED** = planned, not shipped | **UNVERIFIED** = Marshal not certain — MoatGhost confirm

**Reading guide:** Many GDPR obligations apply to **the controller** (the adopter using Unheaded), not to Unheaded itself. Where the obligation is squarely the adopter's, Marshal flags it `ADOPTER-OWNS` rather than GAP — that's an information-architecture statement, not a missed control.

## Headline gaps (priority order for MoatGhost)

1. **Records of Processing Activities (Art. 30)** — 0% coverage. Single-page document, high-leverage.
2. **DPA template** for adopters who insist on processor-of-record — even a thin "we see no PII" attestation DPA closes Art. 28.
3. **Privacy policy publication + DSR contact** (Art. 12-15) — close the surface, even if every response is "we never had your PII."
4. **Breach response runbook** with 72-hour notification path (Art. 33). Tonight's `runbooks/security/` already has skeletons; one needs to specifically cover GDPR breach notification.
5. **DPIA template** (Art. 35) — ship one DPIA for the eBPF tracing pipeline as the worked example; it's the highest-residual-risk processing.
6. **Retention policy per data class** (Art. 5(1)(e)) — The Well (PostgreSQL), Anamnesis Lite traces, Wotan topic logs, Sophia dictionaries each need a documented retention.

## What's load-bearing on the architectural posture

The kingdom's **strongest GDPR story** is structural, not procedural: zero-customer-data-access by design (Art. 25), eBPF metadata-only tracing (Art. 5(1)(c) data minimization), TLS 1.3 + ML-DSA-65 (Art. 5(1)(f) integrity & confidentiality), Champion-gated mutations + audit (Art. 32 security). When MoatGhost authors the GDPR narrative, lead with these — the procedural gaps above are real but easier to close than re-architecting.
