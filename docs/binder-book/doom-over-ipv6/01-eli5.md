# Doom Over IPv6: Explain Like I'm Five

## The Toy Train Analogy

Imagine you have a toy train track that goes in a circle. The track passes through six little stations. At each station, there's an elf who knows how to do math — adding, subtracting, moving numbers around.

Now imagine you want to play a video game, but the only workers you have are these six elves, and the only way to tell them "do some work" is to send the toy train past their station.

**Every time the train passes an elf, the elf does a tiny bit of work** — like coloring in one tiny dot on a picture, or figuring out where a monster should move.

When the elf is done with their tiny bit of work, they put the train back on the track and it rolls to the next station. The train goes around and around and around, and each time it passes an elf, the picture gets a little more complete.

## So Where's the Video Game?

The video game isn't *on* the train. The train is empty! It's just a signal that says **"Hey elf, wake up and do your next bit of work!"**

All the actual game stuff — the picture of the monsters, the map of the level, the rules for how the game works — is written down in big books that all six elves can read. The books sit on a shared shelf that every station can reach.

The elves read from the books, do a tiny bit of work, then scribble their results (like "this pixel should be green") onto a shared drawing pad.

Meanwhile, there's a kid with a camera (that's the "bridge") who takes a photo of the drawing pad about 60 times every second and shows it on a TV screen. That's how you see Doom!

## Why a Train?

Because the elves only wake up when the train goes by! No train = no work = frozen picture.

A special helper (the "injector") keeps putting new trains on the track so the elves never run out of work. More trains = faster game.

## The Whole Thing in One Sentence

**A tiny empty message goes around in a circle, and every time it passes a checkpoint, a little worker does a small piece of the Doom video game, and a camera watches the shared drawing pad to show you the picture.**
