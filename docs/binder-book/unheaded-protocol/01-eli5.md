# The Unheaded Protocol: Explain Like I'm Five

## The Magic Sticker

Imagine you're sending a letter to your friend. Normally, the mailman just carries the letter — he doesn't read it or write on it.

But what if the mailman had a **magic sticker** on every letter? And at every house the mailman passes, someone peeks at the sticker, writes a tiny note on it ("I saw this letter at 2pm! It looked healthy!"), and sends it along.

By the time the letter arrives, the sticker has notes from *every single house* it passed through. You know exactly where it went, how long each part took, and if anything went wrong.

**That's the Unheaded Protocol.** Every message inside the system gets a little 20-byte sticker (called the **Monad**), and every stop it passes through reads the sticker and writes on it.

## The Kingdom

The sticker only exists inside the **Kingdom** — the private network where all the computers trust each other. When a message arrives from the outside world, a gatekeeper called **Shield** sticks the magic sticker on it. When a message leaves the Kingdom, Shield peels the sticker off. The outside world never sees it.

```
Outside World (no sticker)
       |
   [ Shield ] ← puts sticker ON
       |
   The Kingdom (every message has a sticker)
       |
   [ Shield ] ← peels sticker OFF
       |
Outside World (no sticker)
```

## The Dictionary

The sticker doesn't use regular words. It uses **secret codes** — just numbers. The number `7` might mean "this came from the Captain service" and `3` might mean "this is super important."

A wise librarian called **Sophia** keeps the codebook. If you want to know what a number means, you ask Sophia. And here's the cool part: you can change the codebook *without changing any stickers*. The same number `7` could mean something different tomorrow if Sophia says so.

## The Memory

Every time someone reads a sticker, they also write a note in a big **diary** called **Anamnesis** (that means "remembering"). The diary records everything that happens to every message. If something goes wrong, you can look back through the diary and see exactly what happened, where, and when.

## The Boss

There's a boss called **Wotan** who sits in the middle. Wotan speaks two languages:
- **Computer language** (the tiny fast codes on the stickers)
- **People language** (the dashboards and reports humans read)

Wotan reads the diary, translates the codes using Sophia's codebook, and tells the dashboards what's happening. Wotan also takes orders from the humans and writes new rules into the codes.

## The Whole Thing in One Sentence

**Every message gets a tiny sticker with secret codes, every stop reads and writes on it, a diary remembers everything, and a translator in the middle turns the codes into something humans can understand.**
