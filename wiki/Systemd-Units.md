## Start limits

Every unit that restarts itself can also give up (ADR-147 SS3, S-SEE-AND-SURVIVE Phase 6). systemd's default limit is 5 starts in 10 s, which a unit with `RestartSec` of 5 s or more can never reach, so a unit failing at every start restarted for ever and nobody was told (on west, wiki-server restarted 15 times at boot on 2026-11-12). Each restarting unit in `deploy/systemd/` carries, in `[Unit]`, `StartLimitIntervalSec=30min` and `StartLimitBurst` = 900 / `RestartSec` (180 at 5 s, 90 at 10 s, 30 at 30 s): a unit failing at every start keeps trying through a 15-minute boot window (dockerd took 3 minutes on 2026-11-12; the Well, wg0 and the compose bridge come after it), then stays `failed`, and UnitFailed (from `unheaded-host-state`) mails. Until the review of 2026-11-15 it was 10 starts in 15 minutes, which gave up after 50 to 300 s. The trace collector keeps its own 600 s / 60 (Stevie, 2026-11-09). `scripts/check-unit-start-limits.sh` fails a restarting unit whose interval is not longer than burst x `RestartSec` (the limit never trips) or whose burst x `RestartSec` is under 900 s (it trips inside the boot window) (CI: `security.yml`; registered in `check-gates-can-fail.sh`). Installed units that have drifted from the repository get a drop-in, `deploy/systemd/dropins/10-start-limit.conf` → `/etc/systemd/system/<unit>.d/10-start-limit.conf` (Phase 14): 180 starts in 2 h, which fits their installed `RestartSec` of 5, 10 or 30 s. After the cause is fixed: `sudo systemctl reset-failed <unit>`, then start it.
